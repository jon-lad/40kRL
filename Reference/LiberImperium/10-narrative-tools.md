                         “Only the insane have strength enough to prosper; only those that prosper truly judge what is sane.”
                                                                                                                                                -Anonymous

In their service to Mankind, one may face innumerable horrors—more than enough to shred the sanity and soul of a normal man. In combating the
servants of Chaos, one risks corruption from the very forces they seek to defeat. Being present at profane ceremonies, reading forbidden texts, and
being exposed to the powers of the Warp can corrupt the body and soul. Characters will encounter many horrific sights, from grisly murders to ter-
rifying xenos creatures. Fear can be a powerful obstacle to overcome, and repeated exposure to experiences of this sort slowly but surely erodes the
sanity of even the strongest of wills.
           The following rules provide game mechanics to represent the threats to mind and soul which characters must face. These rules are separated
into two primary sections: dealing with Fear, and dealing with Insanity and Corruption. Fear concerns the character’s immediate, visceral response to
horrifying events, creatures, and experiences. Insanity explores the long-term effects of fears; even after he overcomes his fear, the damage to his psy-
che remains, and as horror piles upon horror, he may eventually succumb to madness. Even more insidious, Corruption represents the lasting effects
of exposure to Chaos, the Warp, and revelations of forbidden knowledge that can ruin a soul. Even the stoutest heart cannot face exposure to such
unnatural terrors unchanged, and though the effects can remain hidden for many years, eventually slow corruption can damn a character as surely as
an unholy daemonic bargain.
           During combat encounters, only the rules for Fear are generally used. The effects of Insanity and Corruption are usually dealt with in the
aftermath of a battle rather than during it.


                                                  Insanity and Corruption Points
Insanity Points (InsP) and Corruption Points (CorrP) are characteristics that increase for PC’s over the course of play. Both of these scores begin at 0
when a PC begins the game and increase over time as damage is done to a character’s state of mind (as demonstrated by Insanity points) and the pu-
rity of his soul (as demonstrated by Corruption points). The higher these scores, the more tainted, damaged, and debilitated a character becomes, and
should either of these scores ever reach 100, the character is either slain for his own good, or falls from grace and becomes an enemy of Mankind. In
either case, he can no longer serve the Master of Mankind—he is lost in the eyes of the Emperor and must be retired from play.

The nightmarish galaxy is inimical to humanity, and thus Acolytes, Explorers, and Guardsmen must battle against terrifying aliens, perversions of
science, unholy entities from beyond the veil, and innumerable other horrors in the course of their duties. The mere knowledge that such things ex-
ist is deathly terrifying to the average citizen of the Imperium. In the course of their adventures, characters come face-to-face with horrific creatures
and experience terrifying events, from bloody mass sacrifices in the name of dark gods to impossible xenos monstrosities from the darkest void.
Overcoming their fear in order to fight on against such terrors can be amongst the greatest challenges characters must face.

Shock and Snapping Out of It
Characters can try to shake off some of the effects of Fear after the initial shock has worn off. When specified on the Shock Table a character can
“snap out of it,” he makes a Discipline Test at the beginning of his next turn. If successful, he regains his senses, shrugs off the effects, and can act
normally. If he fails, the effect continues and the character can try to snap out of it again at the beginning of his following turn, and so on.

Removing Insanity
With the GMs permission, a character may remove Insanity Points by
spending his downtime in quiet, familiar, or even pleasant surroundings.
It takes 1 week of time to remove a single Insanity Point. A character
cannot ever go down a Level of Mental Affliction, and so can never lose
one of his disorders through this method. The removal of Insanity points
should be properly represented as time and effort spent by the character
in game (long-term palliative care, prayer, fasting, penance, recuperation
in quiet and pleasant surroundings, etc.).

                                                                  Mental Trauma
Mental Trauma represents the relatively short-term damage to a character’s state of mind that he suffers after experiencing a particularly horrific
event. Each time the character’s Insanity Point total rises by 10 points, he must make a Trauma test. This is a Discipline Test, modified in difficulty
by how many Insanity points the character has accrued in total. If the character passes the test, he manages to cope with his experience without any ill
effect. If he fails the test, roll on the Mental Traumas Table, adding +10 to the roll for every Degree of Failure. This result is applied in the aftermath
of any encounter that inflicted the Insanity points.

A character’s Corruption points total is also used to determine the terrible effects of Chaos upon his body. As his Corruption builds, his flesh may
revolt, twisted by the Dark Gods. Of course, such is the insidious nature of Chaos that it is constantly hunting for weakness in mind and body,
testing a character’s defences until it can find a way into his soul. At 20, 50, and 80 Corruption Points a character must make a test against two
Characteristics of his choice. He can never test against the same Characteristics twice to resist mutation, and should make a note on his Character
Sheet of which Characteristics he has already used to resist mutation. Failure on both testing both Characteristics means he suffers a mutation. The
unfortunate soul rolls a 1d100 to gain a random mutation from the Minor Mutations Table at 20 and 50 Corruption, and upon the Major Mutations
Table at 80 Corruption. Should this give him a mutation he already possesses, he must roll again until it results in a fresh mutation.
          Alternatively, When the character would test to gain a mutation as a result of increasing Corruption, he may choose to automatically fail
the Test. If he does, he instead automatically gains a Malignancy and increases his Corruption total by 1d5. If a character has a Fate Characteristic
he may choose to lose 10 Fate to not gain a Minor Mutation, 20 Fate to not gain a Major Mutation, and 30 Fate to not gain a Superior Mutation.

                                   Influence, Profit Factor, and Location
The Imperium is a fractured empire, spread thinly across the immensity of the galaxy. While worlds within a single system can communicate via a
number of conventional methods, interstellar communication requires messengers or Astropaths, methods which are usually costly, slow, or unreli-
able, and often all three. Further, whether astropathically transmitted or carried by a courier aboard a voidship, interstellar communication must rely
on the vagaries of the Warp, with all the unpredictability and danger that entails.
           To represent this a character’s Influence counts as half if they have no Peers in the system or upon the planet they are operating on, though
due to the more universal appeal of wealth Profit Factor only takes a consistent -10 penalty under such circumstances.
However, despite these benefits, Profit Factor is binary, it can or cannot use it, depending on location and communication enabling the use of funds.
If the locale has some form of communication with the wider Imperium, and most specifically mercantile houses, the Administratum, or some other
financial institution then Profit Factor can definitely be used. But beyond the bounds of the Imperium, and even upon poorly developed worlds, such
as frontier, feudal, or colonial worlds such coin is useless, and one must trade upon their reputation, for fair or for foul.
           A messenger can be employed but adds a significant amount of time to Acquisitions, the additional time being equal to necessary travel
time to and from the nearest system where the desired goods are available. Multiple Acquisitions can be made using this method, but any one fail by
more than 4 Degrees of Failure or a 100 be rolled for the Acquisition Test then the entire shipment becomes lost, either to the vagaries of the Warp or
else more corporeal concerns, alongside any necessary Misfortunes.
           If the character is in territory controlled largely or utterly by a group for whom he has the Hatred, Enemy, or Hunted Talent for and he
fails an Acquisition Test in such enemy territory with a modifier of worse than +20 by four or more Degrees of Failure, the player must roll on the
Misfortune table. If they are in an area where both their Hunted and either the Hatred or Enemy Talent apply they have a Misfortune occur on a failed
Acquisition roll of 40+ or worse.
